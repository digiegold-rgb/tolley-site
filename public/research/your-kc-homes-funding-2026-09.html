Loans · Missouri and local lenders

Three practical options to compare

Missouri MOBUCK$ — ask your bank first

State interest subsidy · availability must be confirmed

This is a repayable bank loan with a state-supported rate reduction. The Treasurer announced a $100 million small-business reopening on April 1, 2026, available until funds are depleted, and described rate reductions of approximately 2–3 percentage points. That announcement does not prove funds remain available today. 2026 opening announcement.

Published requirements include Missouri headquarters and operations, fewer than 100 full-time employees, for-profit status, no unpaid non-protested taxes and no unresolved state environmental compliance issues. Listed uses include equipment, inventory, operating expenses, facility repairs, and land/buildings. Your Missouri location is promising; the lender must assess the actual business and use. Eligibility and uses.

The bank approves the loan under normal underwriting standards and submits the state application. New borrowers need a valid Missouri tax-clearance certificate. Ask for the dollar limit, term, fees, collateral, guarantee, rate-subsidy duration and rate after it expires. Application process. Commerce Bank and Country Club Bank appear on the participating lender list; begin with your existing bank if it participates.

AltCap — local community-development lender

Repayable financing · direct inquiry available

AltCap’s Missouri page advertises $1,000–$350,000, fixed rates priced from prime plus 4–6 percentage points, and terms up to five years. It lists 2–5% origination fees and a $15 monthly servicing fee. These are published terms, not a quote; confirm the current product and all-in cost.

Its broad Missouri lending coverage makes this a useful conversation for appliance purchases, repair tools and growth working capital. Ask specifically whether it finances equipment rented to households and how it evaluates real-estate activities inside the same LLC. Contact 833-549-2890 or info@altcap.org. AltCap Missouri terms, documents and application.

Kiva — small, interest-free crowdfunding loan

Repayable financing · application available

Kiva advertises typical U.S. loans of $1,000–$15,000 with 0% interest, no fees, no collateral and no minimum credit score. You must recruit initial lenders from your network before public fundraising; approval and fundraising can take several weeks. Repayment generally starts one month after disbursement, with terms usually 12–36 months.

Potential fit: a small appliance batch with a measurable household-service story. Eligibility includes business-purpose and industry restrictions, a PayPal account and restrictions concerning current bankruptcy, foreclosure or liens. Debt refinancing and purchases of stock/equity are excluded. Ask Kiva to screen your mixed real-estate/appliance business before spending time on the profile. Kiva application and full eligibility FAQ.

Federal loans · The real-estate distinction

Match the money to the actual activity

SBA rules exclude passive landlord/developer businesses that do not actively use or occupy the financed assets, subject to a defined Eligible Passive Company exception. Speculative businesses are also excluded. A lender must review the complete structure and activities. 13 CFR 120.110.

SBA microloan — a sensible small expansion inquiry

Up to $50,000 through nonprofit intermediary lenders. SBA lists working capital, inventory, supplies and equipment as permitted uses; real-estate purchases and existing-debt repayment are excluded. The maximum term is seven years, with rates generally 8–13%, set by the intermediary. Use SBA’s directory to find a lender serving Jackson County and request a quote for the proposed WD purchases. SBA microloans and intermediary directory.

SBA 7(a) — for a larger, supportable growth request

The program generally supports loans up to $5 million for eligible operating businesses. Uses include working capital, equipment, qualifying debt refinancing and business real estate. You apply through a lender and must demonstrate creditworthiness and repayment capacity; the government guarantee protects the lender and does not erase your repayment obligation. Your two-plus-year history helps provide evidence, but does not establish approval or loan size. SBA 7(a) overview and Lender Match.

SBA 504 — a later option for premises or major fixed assets

Explore this with a Certified Development Company if you need an operating facility or qualifying long-lived equipment. SBA describes 10-, 20- and 25-year maturities and requires at least ten years of remaining useful life for financed machinery/equipment. Working capital, inventory and investment rental real estate are excluded. Ordinary used household appliance stock is therefore a weak 504 starting point. SBA 504 requirements.

Show lenders separate revenue, costs, assets and debts for WD and real estate. A new DBA or label alone does not change eligibility; ask whether the actual ownership and entity structure needs professional review.

Grants, incentives and business assistance

Worth pursuing—and where to save time

NASE Growth Grant — up to $4,000

Private competitive grant · applications accepted year-round

NASE requires paid membership in good standing through award. Annual members can apply immediately; monthly members generally need 90 days. Its published schedule reviews July–September applications in October. If eligible, submit a completed application by the end of September for that review cycle; confirm submission details in the member portal.

Potential uses include marketing, part-time help, software and other specific growth needs. Debt and rent/mortgage payments are excluded. Submit a clear use-of-funds statement, business plan, recent tax return or profit-and-loss statement, and photo. A focused WD delivery/repair or customer-growth project is a reasonable proposal. Membership does not guarantee an award; compare dues with the benefits before joining. NASE rules and application.

State and federal programs with narrower fits

  • Missouri Works: hiring/investment incentives rather than an unrestricted grant. Statewide Works lists ten new jobs; Zone Works can start at two jobs plus $100,000 investment in an eligible zone. Jackson County is excluded from Rural Works. Obtain state approval before project commitments covered by its timing rules. Lower priority unless you have a substantial hiring plan. Requirements.
  • Missouri Veterans and Job Opportunity Grant: the official page says applications are closed. It links awards to qualifying hiring and previously claimed federal WOTC. Do not budget an award or assume a future cycle will reopen. Current status.
  • Missouri SSBCI / MTC IDEA Fund: primarily technology-focused venture investment with co-investment, not a general small-business cash grant. A normal appliance-rental expansion is a weak fit. MTC program.
  • USDA Rural Business Development Grants: direct applicants are eligible public entities, tribes and nonprofits serving rural areas—not an ordinary for-profit LLC. USDA eligibility.

Check cash flow before choosing a loan

At a hypothetical 12% annual interest rate over 60 months, a $25,000 amortizing loan costs about $556 per month before fees. This is a calculation example, not a market quote. At an assumed $35 monthly contribution per occupied set after direct costs, 16 occupied sets would cover only that payment. A planning cushion of 1.25 times the payment would require about 20 occupied sets; the lender may use a different standard.

At 80% occupancy, twenty purchased sets produce sixteen occupied sets. Under those assumptions, the expansion would leave almost no cushion for debt, and fixed overhead still must be paid. Use actual collected rent, maintenance, delivery, storage, insurance, idle time, losses and replacement needs; gross rental revenue is not available cash.

Action checklist

Your next 30 days

  1. Days 1–3: define the request. Choose the amount, purchases and timing. Split WD and real-estate revenue and costs. Document current sets, occupied sets, collected monthly revenue, repair costs and waiting customers.
  2. Days 3–7: get an eligibility screen. Contact UMKC SBDC and your bank. Ask the bank about MOBUCK$ allocation availability and repayment requirements. Ask AltCap about equipment rented to households and mixed-activity eligibility.
  3. Days 7–14: compare written terms. Obtain two comparable offers or preliminary term sheets. Compare APR or equivalent all-in cost, origination and servicing fees, payment frequency, term, guarantees, collateral, prepayment costs and net cash received.
  4. By September 30, if eligible: consider submitting a NASE proposal for the published October review cycle. Do not rush into membership solely because a grant is possible.
  5. Days 14–30: select a supportable first phase. Confirm the payment works with conservative occupancy and repair assumptions. If it does not, reduce the first purchase or improve the operating economics before adding debt.

Documents to assemble

  • LLC formation and operating agreement; current ownership details and any DBAs.
  • EIN confirmation letter and required licenses/insurance.
  • Two years of business and personal tax returns, with schedules.
  • Year-to-date profit and loss, balance sheet and business debt schedule.
  • Recent business bank statements; lender-requested history.
  • Cash-flow forecast using actual WD occupancy and costs.
  • Asset list, equipment liens and purchase quotes.
  • Missouri tax clearance for MOBUCK$; lender-requested personal financial statement.
