Why this is priced low

  • $108/sf above grade. The two cheapest sales per square foot in Tomasha Village in twelve months were $106.55 and $107.34 — and the MLS flagged both of those homes "Fixer Up." This subject is being offered below the neighborhood's fixer floor.
  • The closest analog sold $21,000 over ask. 14301 E Covington — also 1.5-story, also as-is, 160 sf smaller, and with an unfinished basement — listed at $270,000 and closed at $291,000 sight unseen in June.
  • Both same-street sales were smaller ranches. 4329 and 4320 S Dover closed at $300,000 and $268,000 with 1,926 and 1,795 finished feet and no finished basement. This subject offers 2,306 above grade plus 690 finished below, and an extra full bath.
  • Nothing competes with it right now. Exactly one active listing in the whole subdivision, at $399,000. Of the three homes under contract, the only one priced below this subject needs extensive remodeling, has a deck the listing calls hazardous, and still drew multiple offers in two days at $199,500.

What could be wrong with this picture

  • Cash and conventional financing only. The listing excludes FHA and VA. That narrows the buyer pool, but it also hints at condition the seller expects an FHA appraiser to flag — most likely the basement bathroom the remarks describe as open and not enclosed.
  • Square footage is from public records, not measured. The listing agent asks buyers to verify. If the true above-grade area comes in near 2,000 sf, the indicated value drops roughly $12,000 — it does not change the conclusion.
  • Sold as-is, seller will not repair. A 1971 home with unknown roof, HVAC and electrical age. Two comparables disclosed exactly these surprises — a foundation repair and a replaced electrical panel.
  • The margin absorbs a lot. It would take roughly $57,000 of deferred maintenance and repair before $250,000 stops being a discount and becomes merely fair.

Offer strategy

  • Expect competition, and expect it fast. Ten of the last fifteen sales here went under contract within fifteen days; seven within five. Under-priced as-is homes in this subdivision have gone to contract at zero days on market and closed over ask. The listing requires 1% earnest money and a 48-hour offer expiration — that is a listing agent set up to collect and compare offers, not to negotiate slowly.
  • An offer in the $265,000–$285,000 range still clears the analysis with roughly $25,000 to $45,000 of headroom against the indicated $308,800. Coming in at list is unlikely to win.
  • Ask for inspection, not repairs. The seller has stated they will not repair. An inspection for information — with a walk-away right rather than a repair request — keeps the offer competitive while protecting against the roof/HVAC/foundation unknowns.
  • Concessions are normal here. Ten of fifteen sellers paid them, median $5,900 among those who did. A cash-equivalent price matters more than the headline number.
